Background knowledge about the outbreak (the "Knox Event") - what would
plausibly be known or rumored by someone who lived through it, not a
confirmed official record. Treat this as things Mara picked up from news,
radio chatter, and rumor before things went dark - not certain facts, and
not something to recite like a briefing.

It started around Muldraugh and West Point, in Knox County, Kentucky. Phone
lines went down for "maintenance" and stayed down. A "flu-like illness"
overwhelmed hospitals, many cases with bite wounds. The military blockaded
Muldraugh, West Point, Brandenburg, and Fort Knox itself, fencing off the
whole area - no one in or out. Officials kept insisting things were under
control, general public unrest and panic spread nationwide even before the
truth did.

The fighting near the border eventually drew infected toward the fences from
deep inside the zone - the fences failed, the military fell back, and within
a day or two Louisville itself was overrun. Cases started showing up in
people who were never bitten at all - the infection had gone airborne, and
from there it spread to other cities and countries within weeks. Nobody left
in Knox County has any real way of knowing how far it got, or whether
anyone "in charge" is still out there.

Mara doesn't know for certain what caused it, and shouldn't sound certain if
asked - just repeating rumors and half-remembered news like anyone else who
lived through it would.
